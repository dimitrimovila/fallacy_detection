# Spec 04. Interviewer, serving, minimal parser and pilot

Version 4.13, 4 October 2026. Components: `src/argfallacy/client/`, `src/argfallacy/parse/` (minimal version), `src/argfallacy/eval/pilot.py` (the pilot report), `serving/` (with `serving/serve_command.py` and the jobs in `serving/slurm/`), `.gitattributes`.
Depends on: 01, 03 and the data (`docs/data.md`). Produces: `runs/<run_id>/`.

## 1. Serving on the cluster (`serving/`)

* `serving/models.yaml`: one entry per model with `tier`, `model_id` (the name vLLM serves it under), `revision` (the Hugging Face commit), `display_name`, `enabled`, `supports_logprobs`, `is_reasoning`, `reasoning`, `max_concurrency`, `serve_args`, `notes`, the sampling of the entry (`samples`, `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`) and optionally `max_tokens`. Tier 1: Qwen3.8 27B, Gemma 4 31B, gpt oss 20b; tier 2: gpt oss 120b, K2 Horizon 32B; tier 3: GPT 5.5, disabled. The exact names of the Hugging Face repositories are fixed in `serving/models.yaml`, and so are the revisions: a `main` commit for every open model, checked on 24 September 2026, the same for the two entries of a model. K2 Horizon is pinned to the head of `main` on 28 September 2026, commit `eff3d24a3a40` of 24 September, which removed from the model card the announcement of a final checkpoint; the history of the repository keeps the intermediate checkpoints `sft_1_*`. While a revision is `PLACEHOLDER` the client refuses to call that model.
* Reasoning conditions. Qwen3.8 and Gemma 4 switch reasoning on and off over the same weights, so each of them has two entries with the same `model_id` and `revision` and a different `reasoning`: `qwen3_8_27b` and `gemma4_31b` with `enable_thinking: false`, `qwen3_8_27b_think` and `gemma4_31b_think` with `enable_thinking: true` and the template's default level. The main condition is reasoning off. The `reasoning` goes into the cache key, so the two entries share no answers.
* The `max_tokens` of an entry applies instead of the configuration's. The entries with reasoning off have 1024: in the first pilot the 99th percentile of their answers was 115 tokens, and the only two answers cut off at 512 were justifications turned into reasoning. The entries with reasoning on need more, since they write their reasoning before the JSON: 8192 for the `_think` entries, 32768 for K2 Horizon and the two gpt oss entries, whose reasoning does not switch off (for gpt oss the effort takes only low, medium or high; for K2 Horizon it is set to `high`, the default of its template and the only level of its model card). Being a generation parameter, it goes into the cache key and into the manifest.
* Samples per entry. Every entry that is planned states how many answers it gives to every question and how each is drawn: `samples`, `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`, the same for stage one and stage two and for every sample, with the seed equal to the sample index. `top_k: 0` switches top-k off in vLLM 0.30.0 (`SamplingParams`). The entries of Qwen and Gemma with reasoning off take one sample at temperature 0, top_p 1; gpt oss 20b and 120b five at temperature 1, top_p 1 (OpenAI's recommendation); K2 Horizon five at temperature 1, top_p 0.95 (its model card). An entry without the four fields cannot be planned: the `_think` entries have none, and are out of the second pilot. The number of samples of the entry replaces the single number of the configuration.
* vLLM version: 0.30.0 for every model and every entry, installed with pip in a conda environment of its own. It is the first version that supports K2 Horizon (reasoning and tool parsers), and it serves all the models of the plan. The standard wheel is built for CUDA 13.0 and needs an NVIDIA driver 580 or later; with an older driver the CUDA 12.9 variant of the same version is installed. Before installing, a short interactive job on the node reads its driver with `nvidia-smi`, and the driver chooses the wheel. For the CUDA 12.9 variant the command of the vLLM documentation (the `+cu129` wheel with `--extra-index-url`) left on the cluster a PyTorch built for CUDA 13.0 and the standard vLLM of PyPI, which does not import; the README gives the sequence that gave a working environment (PyTorch `+cu129` from the PyTorch index, then the `+cu129` wheel of vLLM with `--no-deps`, then `cuda-python~=12.9.0`) and the check (`pip show vllm` gives `0.30.0+cu129`, `pip check` clean, the import prints `0.30.0 2.13.0+cu129 12.9`). Wheels built for CUDA 12 and for CUDA 13 are not mixed in one environment: the `-cu12` and `-cu13` packages of cuDNN, NCCL, NVSHMEM and cuSPARSELt write the same files, so removing the `-cu13` ones removes libraries of PyTorch, and neither `pip check` nor the `RECORD` files show it (`ldd` on `libtorch_cuda.so` does); the README gives the repair, reinstalling the `-cu12` ones. The conda environments take Python from conda-forge alone (`-c conda-forge --override-channels`): on the cluster the Terms of Service of the default Anaconda channels are not accepted, and `conda create` stops on them.
* Serving flags in one place. Every entry served with vLLM has `serve_args`, the list of the `vllm serve` flags that depend on the entry: for the reasoning-off entries of Qwen and Gemma, none of the reasoning flags; `--reasoning-parser qwen3` or `--reasoning-parser gemma4` and `--max-model-len 16384` for the `_think` entries; `--trust-remote-code`, `--reasoning-parser k2_horizon`, `--tool-call-parser k2_horizon`, `--max-model-len 34816` and `--chat-template-content-format string` for K2 Horizon, whose chat template reads the content of a message only when it is a string, while vLLM detects its format as `openai` and would pass a list of parts, so the prompt would reach the model empty; `--max-model-len 34816` and `--reasoning-parser openai_gptoss` for the two gpt oss entries, since vLLM 0.30.0 moves the JSON schema to the `final` channel of the harmony format, leaving the `analysis` channel free for the reasoning, only when a parser is configured (`_adjust_output_format` in `vllm/parser/harmony.py`, called from the branch `parser is not None` of `vllm/renderers/online_renderer.py`), and without it the schema constrains the JSON from the first token and the model does not reason. Every entry served with vLLM except the `_think` ones also has `--generation-config vllm`, so that the `generation_config.json` of the model (Qwen3.8: top_k 20, top_p 0.95; Gemma 4: top_k 64, top_p 0.95) does not put its sampling values in place of those of the request, and `--structured-outputs-config '{"backend": "xgrammar", "disable_any_whitespace": true}'`, so that the JSON has a single layout, `{"answer": "` with separators `", "` and `": "`, and the context before the answer token is the same in every call: in the first pilot, without the option, xgrammar let the model choose the whitespace, and three layouts of the prefix changed the masses of the answer between the samples of one prompt. With gpt oss the option does not reach the grammar: vLLM 0.30.0 turns the schema into a structural tag of the harmony format, which xgrammar 0.2.8 compiles with free whitespace (vLLM issue 54497). The layout matters where the soft answer is the logprobs, the entries with one sample; for the entries with five the soft answer is the frequency over the samples, and in the first pilot the final masses of the entries that reasoned were above 0.99 in every question. So the layout stops only the entries with one sample (smoke test below), and the pilot report gives the share of the layout for every entry and stage. The context of K2 Horizon and gpt oss is the longest prompt of the v2 templates plus the 32768 of `max_tokens`, rounded up to a multiple of 1024: the longest prompt, over every item of `items.csv` at stage one and every item, scheme and CQ at stage two, measured with the chat template of each model, is 1238 tokens for gpt oss (stage one) and 1161 for K2 Horizon, so 34816 for both. The commercial model has no `serve_args`: it is not served with vLLM. The flags are not read from the `notes`, which keep only the explanation. `serve_args` goes into the manifest, next to `revision`, `tier` and `reasoning`, and into the cache key: the same request to a server launched with other flags is another call.
* `serving/serve_command.py ENTRY` prints, on one line, the part of the `vllm serve` command that comes from `models.yaml`: `<model_id> --revision <revision>` followed by `serve_args`, and `--max-model-len 8192` when `serve_args` does not set it. It refuses an entry whose revision is `PLACEHOLDER`, or that has no `serve_args`, printing nothing on the standard output and exiting with a code other than zero. It reads the YAML with PyYAML alone, so it runs in the environment of the server as well as in that of the client. The caller adds what depends on the node: port, host, `--tensor-parallel-size`, `--dtype auto`.
* `serving/README.md`: a runbook, in the order in which the steps are run. Installing vLLM as above, with both commands. Launching vLLM with the OpenAI-compatible API and structured output enabled, one entry per launch:
  ```
  vllm serve $(python serving/serve_command.py <entry>) --dtype auto --port 8000
  ```
  For Qwen and Gemma the two conditions are served by two launches of the same model, same vLLM version and same weights. The entry with reasoning off is served without a reasoning parser: with the parser on and no reasoning, vLLM from 0.19.0 onwards can silently skip the JSON schema constraint. The `_think` entry is served with vLLM's reasoning parser and with a `--max-model-len` large enough for `max_tokens` plus the prompt (16384): without a parser, structured output constrains the JSON from the very first token and the model cannot reason. gpt oss, whose reasoning does not switch off, is served in the same way, with `--reasoning-parser openai_gptoss` and `--max-model-len 16384`. The run proceeds entry by entry, restarting the server between entries. The smoke test, then `plan` and `execute` with `--model` (section 2.3).
  Then the section on the cluster. No SSH tunnel: server and client run in the same job, and the personal computer can be off. Preparation, once, on the front-end: clone the repository and create `runs/slurm/`, which must exist before a job starts; read the driver of the node with a short interactive job (`srun` on `owner1` with one GPU, `nvidia-smi`) and install vLLM with the matching wheel; create the client environment (`pip install -e .`); set `HF_HOME` in the `.env` of the cluster to a folder under `/extra` (`/storage` is shared by the department and nearly full), and download the weights of the pinned revisions with the Hugging Face command line, for gpt oss without the folders `original/` and `metal/` of its repositories, copies of the weights for the reference implementation and for Apple Metal that vLLM does not read (`--exclude "original/*" --exclude "metal/*"`: 13.8 GB for the 20b and 65.3 GB for the 120b instead of 41.3 and 195.8 GB); for gpt oss also download the vocabularies `o200k_base` and `cl100k_base` into a folder under `/extra` and name it in the `.env` as `TIKTOKEN_ENCODINGS_BASE`, since the `openai-harmony` library through which vLLM reads the harmony format downloads them on first use otherwise, and the job must not depend on the network of the node; `LLM_API_KEY` in the `.env` (any string). The job exports every setting of the `.env` before it launches the server, so `vllm serve` sees them as the client does. Then: `argfallacy run plan configs/pilot.yaml`, a `<run_id>` kept aside for a resubmission, `sbatch serving/slurm/pilot.sbatch <run_id>`, `squeue -u $USER` to follow it, the logs in `runs/slurm/`. No separate smoke test is needed before the pilot job, which runs it on every entry before the calls of that entry; `sbatch serving/slurm/smoke.sbatch ENTRY` tries one entry without creating a run.
* `serving/smoke_test.py`: sends a chat completion with the sampling of the entry, `logprobs=true`, `top_logprobs=20` and `response_format` with the stage-two JSON schema on a test item, and prints the answer, the top_logprobs at the position of the first token of `answer`, and the latency. It compares the `prompt_tokens` of `usage` with an estimate from the prompt sent, four characters per token: fewer than a quarter of the estimate means the chat template dropped the content, and the model answered an empty prompt. If the logprobs do not arrive, it says so plainly. On an entry that reasons, that is with `enable_thinking` true, or with `is_reasoning` true where the entry does not set `enable_thinking` (K2 Horizon, gpt oss), it also says whether there was any reasoning and whether the answer was cut off (`finish_reason` equal to `length`). The reasoning is read from `usage` (reasoning tokens) and from the reasoning field of the message (`reasoning_content` or `reasoning`), since `usage` can report zero while the field holds the thinking; its length and its source are printed, and only zero from both is no reasoning. For K2 Horizon vLLM does not fill `usage.reasoning_tokens` at all, zero even when the model reasons: its reasoning is measured on the text of the field, never on `usage`. Then it sends the same prompt three more times at temperature 1, seeds 1 to 3, and checks that every content (on an entry that reasons, the content after the reasoning parser) begins with `{"answer": "`; on an entry with several samples a different layout is a warning that prints the prefixes seen, and does not stop it. This is the first command to run on every model and on every new entry, and a gate: it exits with a code other than zero, besides a refusal, an error or an answer token missing from the final JSON, when the prompt was not read, when an entry that reasons shows no reasoning or its answer is cut off, when a content of the three does not begin with `{"answer": "` on an entry with one sample, and when an entry with `supports_logprobs: true` receives no logprobs. The messages are the same; the exit code lets a job stop on it.
* The jobs (`serving/slurm/`). A vLLM server loads one model with one set of flags, and the pilot has four entries that need four different launches; two models do not fit together on the two 48 GB GPUs of `labdasan0`. Run as one process against one endpoint, the pilot would fail silently: after the reasoning-off entry of Qwen, the `_think` entry would be sent to the same server, which accepts it (same `model_id`) but has no reasoning parser, so structured output constrains the JSON from the first token and the model does not reason, and the answers would be stored as the reasoning-on condition without any reasoning. So a job runs one entry at a time, restarts the server between entries, and checks every launch with the smoke test before any call.
  `smoke.sbatch ENTRY` and `pilot.sbatch RUN_ID`, submitted with `sbatch` from the root of the repository on the front-end. Header: partition `owner1`, `--gres=gpu:2`, `--cpus-per-task=8`, `--mem=64G`; time 01:00:00 for the smoke test and 10:00:00 for the pilot (the first took about 7 h 50 min over two jobs: a timeout at 4 h, then 3 h 51 min); a job out of time is resubmitted with the same arguments and resumes. Logs in `runs/slurm/`, which git ignores. The steps shared by the two jobs are in `serving/slurm/common.sh`, which both source. In order:
  1. `set -euo pipefail`; conda from the shared installation (`/conf/shared-software/anaconda`); the settings of the `.env` at the root of the repository exported into the shell, read by the client's own reader, so a variable already in the environment wins over the file.
  2. Print `nvidia-smi`, and from the server environment the versions of vLLM and PyTorch, the CUDA version PyTorch was built for and whether it sees the GPUs.
  3. `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1`: the weights of the pinned revisions are downloaded beforehand from the front-end, so the job does not depend on the network of the node. `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_SAMPLER=0`: FlashInfer compiles its sampling kernels on first use with the `nvcc` of the environment, and the environment has none (a CUDA 13 compiler there gave kernels that the driver of `labdasan0`, CUDA 12.9, cannot run); the sampler of PyTorch draws from the same top-k and top-p distribution.
  4. A port derived from the job id, and `LLM_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:<port>/v1` exported for the client, with the server listening only on that address: the node is shared, and a fixed port could collide with another job. The variable of the environment wins over the `.env`, as `load_env` already does.
  5. For each entry: start `vllm serve` in the background with the command of `serve_command.py`, read back as shell words so that a JSON value stays one argument, plus port, host, `--tensor-parallel-size` equal to the GPUs of the job and `--dtype auto`, its output in a log of its own and in a process group of its own; wait for `/health` with a time limit, and stop if the server process exits; run the smoke test on the entry; stop the server and wait until it has released the GPUs. A job that stops half way still stops its server.

  `smoke.sbatch` does step 5 for the entry given, and stops after the smoke test. `pilot.sbatch CONFIG RUN_ID [ENTRY ...]` does step 5 for the entries named, or for every entry of the configuration when none is named, in the order of the configuration, and between the smoke test and the stop runs `argfallacy run execute CONFIG --run-id RUN_ID --model ENTRY`; a name that is not in the configuration stops the job before any server starts. A run made in two times is two jobs with the same `RUN_ID`. If a smoke test fails, the job stops before calling that entry. Calls that fail do not stop it: the job goes on to the next entry and ends with a code other than zero, listing the entries that have failed calls. Resubmitting the job with the same `RUN_ID` resumes from the missing calls.
  Two conda environments: `vllm-0.30.0` for the server and `argfallacy` for the client (`pip install -e .`), so that the dependencies of the client cannot move those of vLLM. The names of the environments are written in the scripts; no personal path is.
* Line endings: `.gitattributes` forces LF on `*.sh` and `*.sbatch` too: the repository is edited on Windows, and a script with CRLF does not run on the cluster.

Environment variables: `LLM_BASE_URL` (for example `http://localhost:8000/v1`; in a job, set by the job), `LLM_API_KEY` (any string for vLLM; the real key for a commercial provider), and on the cluster `HF_HOME` and, for gpt oss, `TIKTOKEN_ENCODINGS_BASE`. None in the code.

## 2. The client (`argfallacy.client`)

### 2.1 Call
A function `ask(request) -> RawResponse` built on the `openai` package (chat completions), with: `model_id`, messages, `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`, `seed`, `max_tokens`, `response_format` (JSON Schema), `logprobs` and `top_logprobs` when the model supports them. The three sampling parameters are always sent, so that nothing is left to the defaults of the server; `top_k`, not a parameter of the OpenAI API, goes in `extra_body`. `RawResponse` keeps the full response exactly as it arrives (content, logprobs block, usage, `finish_reason`), plus latency and timestamp. Never summarised, never cleaned up. What is written to disk is its stored form (section 2.5).

### 2.2 Cache
SQLite in `runs/cache.sqlite`, a table with key = sha256 of (`model_id`, `prompt_version`, text of the rendered prompt, generation parameters, `sample_index`, `revision`, `tier`, `reasoning`, `serve_args`) and value = the serialised `RawResponse`, in the stored form of section 2.5. The generation parameters are `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`, `max_tokens`, `seed`, `logprobs`, `top_logprobs` (only when logprobs are requested) and whether a JSON schema is sent, not the schema itself. `serve_args` is the whole list of the entry: a server launched with other flags gives another key. `model_id` is the identifier the server serves, not the key or the `display_name` of `models.yaml`. The item and CQ identifiers are not in the key: the rendered prompt stands for them, so two items with the same text share their answers, and a template edit invalidates the entry even if the version string was not bumped. The cache is consulted before every call; a call already made is not repeated. The cache is never cleared automatically. Changing the prompt changes the key, so a new prompt version generates new calls only for that prompt.

### 2.3 Plan and execution
* `argfallacy run plan CONFIG` reads a YAML configuration file (items to include, stage, scheme condition `gold` or `predicted`, models, prompt version; the samples are those of each entry) and prints how many calls are needed per model, how many are already in the cache, and an estimate of the duration given the concurrency. It calls nothing.
* `argfallacy run execute CONFIG` creates `runs/<run_id>/` with `run_id` = date and time plus the name of the configuration, writes `manifest.json`, makes the missing calls and appends every response to `raw.jsonl`. Deterministic order: by model, by item, by CQ, by sample.
* `--model NAME`, on `plan` and on `execute`, restricts the plan to the named entry of the configuration. The option can be repeated; the entries run in the order of the configuration. A name that is not among the `models` of the configuration stops the command before any call, with a message that lists the entries of the configuration. Without `--model` nothing changes.
* `--run-id ID` on `execute`: several invocations write into the same run, one folder, one `raw.jsonl`, one manifest. With `--model`, this is how a job that serves one entry per launch of the server writes one run for the whole configuration. The exit code of `execute` is not zero when an entry that the invocation ran has failed calls.
* Concurrency with a limit per model (from `models.yaml`), retries with growing waits on 429 and 5xx errors, a timeout per call. A definitive error produces a line in `raw.jsonl` with `error` set, and the key does not enter the cache, so the call is retried on resume.
* Resume: relaunching the same `execute` after an interruption resumes from the missing calls, without duplicating lines in `raw.jsonl` (only what is not already in the cache is written).
* `predicted` condition: the plan uses the stage-one predictions of the model itself and generates stage-two calls only for the items whose predicted scheme differs from the gold one; for the others the results of the `gold` condition are valid here too, and the parser knows it.

### 2.4 Manifest
`manifest.json` with: `run_id`, full configuration, `model_id` and `display_name`, prompt version, schemes version (tag, the `version` that every YAML of `schemes/` declares and that the files must share, and hash of the content of `schemes/`), the `max_tokens` of the configuration, start and end timestamps, number of calls planned, executed, served from the cache, failed, and per model the note `logprobs_available`, `revision`, `tier`, `reasoning`, `max_tokens`, `samples`, `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k` and `serve_args`.
The manifest describes the whole configuration, whichever entries an invocation executes: the calls planned are those of all the entries; executed, served from the cache and failed are counted over the whole `raw.jsonl` of the run, per entry (`calls_per_model`) and in total; `started_at` is the one of the first invocation, `finished_at` the one of the last. A call counts once: its answered line says executed or served from the cache, and a call with only error lines is failed, so a failure answered on a later try is no longer counted as failed.

### 2.5 `raw.jsonl`
One line per call: `run_id`, `item_id`, `stage`, `scheme_condition`, `scheme`, `cq_id` (empty for stage one), `sample_index`, `model`, `model_id`, `revision`, `tier`, `reasoning`, `prompt_version`, `json_schema` (the schema sent to the model with that call), `params`, `cache_key`, `from_cache`, `raw_response`, `latency_s`, `error`. The file is append-only. It is read one line at a time, never whole, by the resume, by the counts of the manifest and by the parser: the pilot left 13 GB of it. A line ends only at a newline, so a line separator that the JSON leaves inside a string (U+2028, U+0085) does not cut it.
`raw_response`, like the value of the cache, is the stored form of the response: all of it, less two redundancies of the logprobs block, which was most of a line. The copy that the server's payload carries in `raw.choices[0].logprobs`, identical to `logprobs`, is left out, and so is every `bytes` field, the UTF-8 of a token or of an alternative written again as a list of integers. On the pilot, one line in twenty, the two copies were about half a line each and `bytes` 38 to 42 per cent of it: the stored form should be about 70 per cent smaller. The parser reads neither. The cache key and `top_logprobs` do not change. The lines and cache entries written before, the pilot's among them, keep both and stay as they are: they read and parse in the same way.

## 3. Minimal parser (`argfallacy.parse`, pilot version)

`argfallacy parse RUN_ID` reads `raw.jsonl` and produces `answers.csv`, one row per call: the keys above, plus `answer` (canonical or `invalid`), `confidence`, `justification`, `p_logprob` for every admitted answer, `logprob_partial`, `parse_ok`, `finish_reason`. `parse_ok` is true only if the object of the answer respects the `json_schema` of its row (spec 03 section 5): an answer outside the enumeration, with `confidence` outside 0 and 100, missing a required field or with an extra field is `invalid`, and the row stays. Then `summary.csv`, one row per (item, stage, scheme, cq_id, model): hard answer, `p_logprob`, `p_verbal`, `p_sample`, `n_samples_ok`, `idk`, `invalid_count`, according to spec 03 section 6. With one sample the hard answer is sample 0 and `p_sample` is empty; with several, at both stages, it is the majority of the valid samples (parsed, and `finish_reason` other than `length`), a tie going to the answer with the higher mean confidence over the samples that gave it, then to the one given at the lowest sample index, `invalid` with no valid sample, and `p_sample` is the frequency of a yes over the valid samples, counted in `n_samples_ok`. `p_logprob` and `p_verbal` come from sample 0, `p_verbal` oriented on the answer of sample 0. At stage one the admitted answers are the scheme ids, which have no `p_logprob` column: the masses over the schemes are computed and not written, and in `summary.csv` the three probabilities of stage one, oriented on the first scheme id, have no meaning (`docs/proposals.md`). The full version of the parser is spec 05; this one must only suffice for the pilot report and goes no further.

## 4. The pilot

`configs/pilot.yaml`: 50 items chosen from `items.csv` with a gold scheme other than `none`, stratified by scheme in proportion with at least 4 per scheme, fixed seed; stage one and stage two in the `gold` condition; two models, Qwen3.8 27B and Gemma 4 31B, each in the two reasoning conditions, so four entries (`qwen3_8_27b`, `qwen3_8_27b_think`, `gemma4_31b`, `gemma4_31b_think`); five samples. Calls: 6960, per entry 250 of stage one and 1490 of stage two. That was the first pilot, on the prompts v1, with sample 0 at temperature 0 and samples 1 to 4 at 0.7; its configuration is in the manifest of its run. The second pilot is `configs/pilot2.yaml`: the same 50 items (same selection and seed), stage one and stage two in the `gold` condition, the prompts v2 (spec 03), and four entries with the sampling of `models.yaml`: `qwen3_8_27b` and `gemma4_31b`, one sample each, 50 calls of stage one and 298 of stage two; `gpt_oss_20b` and `k2_horizon_32b`, five samples each, 250 and 1490. Calls: 4176. One run, made in two jobs with the same `RUN_ID`: first the two entries with reasoning off, then, if their report holds, the other two.

`items.csv` contains neither the near duplicates nor the removed items (`docs/data.md`, section 5), so the pilot draws from all the items with a gold scheme, that is, the test set of the experiments. The seeded draw runs within each stratum (same scheme).

`argfallacy pilot report RUN_ID` (`src/argfallacy/eval/pilot.py`) writes `pilot_report.md` into the folder of the run. Everything in it is computed from the files the run leaves behind, never by hand: `answers.csv` and `summary.csv` of the parser, which must have run first, `manifest.json`, `data/items.csv`, `data/annotations.csv` and the diagrams of `schemes/`. `raw.jsonl` is read, one line at a time, only for the tokens and the layout of the JSON, which `answers.csv` does not carry. Where it is absent (a copy of the run with only the tables) the report says so in place of those tables and the rest is unchanged; `--no-raw` skips the reading where it is present. The report measures and decides nothing. Everything said below of an answer is said of the hard answer of `summary.csv` (section 3), not of sample 0, except where sample 0 is named. The entries are those of the configuration that have answers in `answers.csv`, so a run made in two jobs is reported after the first too. Per entry:
* integrity: calls, calls with an error, `invalid` answers, truncated answers (`finish_reason` equal to `length`, each also `invalid`), share of rows with `logprob_partial`; and, from `raw.jsonl`, the share of the calls whose content (after the reasoning parser) begins with `{"scheme": "` at stage one and `{"answer": "` at stage two, per entry and stage, expected 100 per cent;
* stage one: samples per question, accuracy of the hard answer on the gold scheme with the canonical scorer and, for an entry with several samples, the share of items where the valid samples agree; accuracy per gold scheme, and a reduced confusion matrix (the errors only);
* for every CQ: distribution of the answers over all the samples, with the shares of `cannot_be_determined`, `na` and `invalid`, and the same three shares over the whole of stage two;
* what each entry answered where another entry answered `cannot_be_determined` on the same item and question: one row per pair of entries, with the number of such questions and the share of each answer given there. It shows whether `cannot_be_determined` follows the question and the text, the other entries not deciding either, or the entry;
* for an entry with several samples, the share of the questions where the valid samples agree;
* Spearman correlation between `p_logprob`, `p_verbal` and `p_sample`, per entry and per CQ, each pair on the rows that have both (`cannot_be_determined` and `na` have no `p_verbal`, an entry with one sample has no `p_sample`); and their spread: share of `p_logprob` above 0.99 or below 0.01, median of the highest `p_logprob` among the admitted answers at sample 0, share of `p_verbal` at or beyond 0.95 and 0.05, share of `p_sample` strictly between 0 and 1;
* verdicts through the diagrams, from the hard answers, under three rules. `diagram`: the hard answers as they are, so `cannot_be_determined`, `na` and `invalid` stop the traversal with no verdict. `logprob`: at every node the arc whose answer has the highest `p_logprob` of sample 0, the mass of the answers outside the arcs ignored (the hard answer where no arc has mass). `toward_good`: an answer outside the arcs takes the arc with the shortest path to `good_argumentation`; where neither arc reaches it (every node of ad hominem, CQ3.1 to CQ3.3 of popular opinion, CQ1.1 of analogy, CQ3.1 of expert opinion) the traversal stays incomplete. Only `diagram` is the diagram: the other two are exploratory readings of an answer the diagram does not draw, not the aggregators of phase 3. Per rule and entry: coverage, accuracy and macro F1 in the fine space with the canonical scorer, where no verdict counts as wrong, and accuracy of fallacy against good argumentation. Beside them, the majority per scheme of spec 06: every item gets the most frequent gold fallacy of its gold scheme on the test set, pilot items included, an oracle on the prior. Then the incomplete traversals of `diagram`, per scheme, with the node where they stop and the answer found there;
* agreement of the hard answer with the per-CQ annotation of the `Annotazione Dumitru` sheets, overall and per CQ, on the pilot items annotated under the same scheme the run used; `idk` of the annotation is compared with `cannot_be_determined`;
* cost: mean and median latency per call and stage; mean tokens per call from `raw.jsonl`, the lines with `error` left out: prompt, completion, reasoning tokens from `usage`, answer tokens (completion less reasoning), and the length in characters of the reasoning field of the message, which holds where `usage` reports no reasoning tokens (for K2 Horizon vLLM leaves `usage.reasoning_tokens` at zero, and its reasoning is measured on that text); the distribution of the output tokens per call (completion, reasoning included: median, 95th and 99th percentile, maximum) and the number of calls with `finish_reason` equal to `length`; and the time projection of the full run: all the items of `items.csv` (today 773) at stage one, the CQs of the items with a gold scheme at stage two in the `gold` condition, the samples of each entry. The hours are calls times mean latency divided by the concurrency of the entry, without loading and smoke tests; the same estimate applied to the pilot is given for comparison;
* parser checks: rows of sample 0 at stage two whose answer is not the answer with the highest `p_logprob` (meaningful at temperature 0), and rows of stage one in `summary.csv` whose `p_sample` is not the frequency of the hard answer over the valid samples. The minimal parser orients `p_sample` and `p_verbal` on the first admitted answer, which at stage one is the first scheme id, so at stage one the two columns have no meaning (`docs/proposals.md`).

Then, for each model run in both reasoning conditions (none in the second pilot), the comparison between the two:
* cost: ratio between the latencies and, where `raw.jsonl` is present, between the completion tokens (reasoning included) of the two conditions, and the time projection of phase 2 with and without the reasoning-on condition;
* agreement between the hard answers of the two conditions, overall, only on the questions where both answered on an arc, and per CQ;
* shape of `p_logprob`: share of values above 0.99 or below 0.01, per CQ and overall. With reasoning on, the answer comes after a text that has already decided it, and the probability could concentrate at the extremes and lose information;
* stage-one accuracy and share of `cannot_be_determined`, side by side; the correlations between the three probabilities are in the table per entry.

The tables per CQ (answers per entry, extreme `p_logprob` and agreement between the conditions, correlations) are in three appendices at the end of the report.

Stopping rule written in the report: every CQ with more than 50 per cent `cannot_be_determined` or more than 10 per cent `invalid` on an entry, over all its samples on all the items, must be listed at the top of the report, with the reason; the same 50 per cent threshold applies to `na`. A share exactly at the threshold is not above it. The decision on what to do is not automatic: it is taken by reading the report. The same holds for the reasoning-on condition: whether it enters phase 2 is decided on the comparison above.

### 4.1 What the first pilot showed about the soft answers

Two read-only checks on the run `20260927T135510Z_pilot`, reasoning off, stage two (298 prompts per entry, five samples each):
* Layout. Without `disable_any_whitespace` the prefix before the answer token took three forms (`{"answer": "`, `{\n"answer": "`, `{\n  "answer": "`); with five samples of a prompt the prefix was the same in only 51 prompts of 298 for Qwen and 95 for Gemma, and a different prefix changed the masses of the answer up to 0.25 (Qwen) and 0.68 (Gemma). Hence the compact layout of section 1.
* Numerical noise of the server. With the same prompt and the same prefix, token by token, the masses still vary between calls, up to 0.034 for Qwen and up to 0.113 for Gemma (5 prompts of 95 above 0.01), and the logprob of the first generated token, which depends on the prompt alone, up to 0.125; the masses of a prompt take two or three distinct values, as from the composition of the batch. vLLM's batch invariance (`VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT`) is not used: its documented requirement is a GPU of compute capability 9.0 or more, and the RTX 6000 Ada of `labdasan0` is 8.9; and the mamba/GDN attention backends of Qwen3.8 declare no support for it. The numbers of the thesis come from the saved archive of the runs, not from new calls.
* `p_logprob` is the probability at temperature 1 over the tokens the grammar admits: the logprobs that vLLM returns are renormalised after the mask of the structured output (the alternatives the grammar excludes have logprob -9999), not the raw distribution of the model; they are computed before the temperature of the request is applied (`logprobs_mode` `raw_logprobs`, the default of vLLM 0.30.0).
* The first token `n` is shared by `no`, `na` and `negative`: the parser counts it for the first of them in the list. Its mass was at most 0.00018 in the pilot, so the answers are not renamed.
* The vocabulary of Gemma has two entries with the same text for `y`, `c` and `n`, both in `top_logprobs`; their masses are negligible (of the order of 1e-8) and the parser sums them with no effect on the result.

## 5. Acceptance tests

Since 20 September 2026 `tests/` keeps only the tests that defend a result, check the data or help the work. The points without a note have an automatic test; the marked points remain requirements, but today they have no test, or only a partial one.

All of them with the fake backend in `tests/fakes/llm.py`, which returns deterministic answers as a function of the prompt and of the `sample_index`, with a realistic logprobs block; no network calls in the tests.

1. Cache: two identical `ask` produce a single call to the backend; changing `prompt_version`, a parameter (`top_p` and `top_k` included), `sample_index` or `serve_args` produces another one. `ask` always sends `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k`.
2. Resume: a run interrupted half way (the fake backend raises an error after N calls) and relaunched completes the plan without duplicate lines in `raw.jsonl` and without redoing the successful calls.
3. Plan: on 10 test items with a known gold scheme, `plan` counts calls = sum over the CQs of the scheme of every item, times the samples of the entry, times the models, plus the stage-one calls. An entry with one sample gives one call per question at its temperature; an entry with five gives five, all at the same temperature, with seed equal to the sample index; an entry without its sampling is refused. On `configs/pilot2.yaml`, 4176 calls.
4. Manifest: all the fields of section 2.4 present; the schemes hash changes if a byte of a YAML file is modified. *Today only the part on the fields has an automatic test.*
5. Parser: from a fake `raw.jsonl` with valid answers, invalid ones and partial logprobs, `answers.csv` and `summary.csv` have the expected values; `p_logprob` is renormalised over the admitted answers; an output that does not respect the schema is `invalid` and does not drop the row. One sample is the hard answer and leaves `p_sample` empty; with several, the majority counts only the valid samples, a tie goes to the higher mean confidence and then to the lower sample index, and no valid sample gives `invalid`. *Today the case with partial logprobs has no automatic test.*
6. `predicted` condition: the plan generates stage-two calls only for the items whose predicted scheme differs from the gold one. *Today without an automatic test.*
7. `smoke_test.py` runs against the fake backend (`--model fake`, the default value) and prints the logprobs block. A server that lays out the JSON with a newline fails the layout check on an entry with one sample and only warns on one with five; a compact one passes. With `--fake-reasoning` the fake puts its thinking in the `reasoning` field of the message, as vLLM does with a reasoning parser, and keeps its tokens in the logprobs, so the smoke test passes. Its checks on reasoning and truncation run on the `_think` entries, K2 Horizon and gpt oss, and not on the reasoning-off entries of Qwen and Gemma. Reasoning in the field of the message with `usage` at zero passes, no reasoning in either fails; a prompt read as a handful of tokens fails. *The run against the fake is by hand; the choice of the entries, the two sources of the reasoning and the check on the prompt have automatic tests.*
8. `max_tokens`: an entry of `models.yaml` with `max_tokens` produces calls with that value instead of the configuration's, and a cache key different from that of an identical entry without it.
9. Equivalence: with the fake backend, four invocations of `execute` with `--model`, one per entry of `configs/pilot2.yaml` and the same `--run-id`, produce the same lines of `raw.jsonl` (compared by `cache_key`) and the same manifest, timestamps aside, as one invocation without `--model`.
10. An entry not in the configuration stops `plan` and `execute` before any call.
11. `serve_command.py`: for `qwen3_8_27b` and `gemma4_31b` the command has no `--reasoning-parser`; for `qwen3_8_27b_think` and `gemma4_31b_think` it has the parser of the model and `--max-model-len 16384`; an entry whose revision is `PLACEHOLDER` (`fake_unpinned` of `tests/fakes/models.yaml`) is refused, with nothing on the standard output. For the four entries of the second pilot the command has `--generation-config vllm` and the JSON of `--structured-outputs-config` intact after quoting for the shell, and the entries with their own `max_tokens` have a context that is a multiple of 1024 larger than it.
12. `bash -n` on the scripts of `serving/slurm/`. They cannot be run outside the cluster, and no job is launched to test them. *Run by hand: no automatic test.* The exit codes of the smoke test are checked by hand against the fake backend.
13. Reading `raw.jsonl`: `read_raw` returns the first row before decoding the second line, and a line with U+2028 and U+0085 inside a string comes back as one row.
14. Stored form: with a fake backend that returns the copy and `bytes`, the lines of `raw.jsonl` and the cache entries of a run have neither, still have the alternatives and `top_logprobs` 20; a response stored whole in the cache reads back equal, and its whole and stored forms parse to the same row.
15. Pilot report, on a run written by hand (two expert opinion items, one model in both reasoning conditions, five samples): the stopping rule lists a CQ above threshold and not one exactly at it; where the hard answer is `cannot_be_determined`, `diagram` gives no verdict and stops at that node, `toward_good` gives `good_argumentation` and `logprob` the arc of the higher mass; `toward_good` has no arc at any node of ad hominem; no verdict counts as wrong and lowers the coverage; the token counts measure the reasoning on the field of the message where `usage` says zero, and leave out a line with `error`; the CQs above threshold come first in the report, and without `raw.jsonl` the report says so; the verdicts follow the majority where sample 0 differs from it; the distribution of the output tokens and the share of the compact layout per stage come out of a `raw.jsonl` written by hand.

## 6. What not to do
* No parsing inside the client: the client saves, and that is all.
* No automatic cleanup of `runs/`.
* No prompt written in the code: only the templates of `prompts/`.
* No calls to the models in the tests.
* No job launched on the cluster to test the scripts.
* No personal path, user name or key in the scripts or in `models.yaml`.
* No other change to the cache key than those listed in section 2.2.

## 7. Definition of done
Lint and tests green (commands in the README), and `bash -n` on the scripts of `serving/slurm/`; `plan` and `execute` work against the fake backend and against vLLM on the cluster with one model (checked with `smoke_test.py`, through `smoke.sbatch`); `pilot_report.md` produced on 50 items, 2 models and their two reasoning conditions; summary with the numbers of the report and the CQs above threshold. The report of the pilot run `20260927T135510Z_pilot` was produced on 28 September 2026, first without `raw.jsonl`, so without the token counts, which come from the copy of the report on the cluster.
