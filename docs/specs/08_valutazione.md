# Spec 08. Evaluation

Draft, 10 September 2026, updated on 11 September 2026. It holds the requirements already decided and the evaluation protocol. Abstention curves and bootstrap parameters are fixed in P11 and P12.

## Requirements already decided

1. **Test set of the experiments without the near duplicates.** All the metrics of the experiments are computed on the test set of `items.csv`, that is, the rows with `in_test_set` true, which does not contain the five near duplicates (`docs/dati.md`, section 5.2). A near duplicate counted twice weighs double and, if a model gets one text wrong, it usually gets the other one wrong too. The near duplicates enter no experiment. The only exception is the reproduction of the numbers of Eleni's diagnosis, which serves only to validate the scorer and uses all the rows of her `results.csv` files as they are.
2. **Eleni's baseline recomputed on the test set of the experiments.** Eleni's results against which the results of the experiments are compared (pipeline and zero-shot of her four models) are recomputed from her `results.csv` files on the items of `items.csv`, that is, on the same test set as the experiments. The exact reproduction of the numbers of the diagnosis on all her rows remains an internal test of the scorer, which validates its computation, and does not appear in the results. Eleni's two rows with the same text (ids 469 and 495, a single item in `items.csv`, `33f6dfaf9c4e41f1`) become a single row in the baseline: the row with id 469 is kept, in every model and condition. The two rows give a different verdict in one run out of eight (deepseek zero-shot: Appeal to Authority for 469, Ad Populum for 495); in the other seven the verdict is the same.

## Evaluation protocol

It rewrites §7 of the thesis plan as requirements. Numbers checked on 11 September 2026 against `labels/fallacies.yaml` and the frozen copy, and again on 21 September 2026 against `data/items.csv` and `data/annotations.csv`. Unless stated otherwise, every number holds on the test set of the experiments (601 items, requirement 1), and every number that goes into the thesis comes from `argfallacy.eval`.

P1. **Conditions on the scheme.** Every aggregator is evaluated in two conditions: gold scheme, which isolates the aggregation, and scheme predicted by stage one, which measures the full system. The two conditions are always reported separately.

P2. **Label spaces.** Two spaces, defined in `labels/fallacies.yaml` under the key `spaces`:
* `fine`: 25 classes, that is, all the terminals of the diagrams, Good Argumentation included. 23 of them appear in the gold: `definist_fallacy` and `false_attribution` have zero support. The plan said "23 classes", counting the classes with support; the space is defined on the terminals, so the classes are 25, and 23 of them have support.
* `collapsed_symmetric`: 20 classes. The six variants of ad hominem (abusive, circumstantial, guilt by association, tu quoque, ad fidentia, poisoning the well) merge into `ad_hominem`, tu quoque included. 18 of them appear in the gold.
* `collapsed_compat` (21 classes, tu quoque separate) serves only the `compat` mode of the scorer, to reproduce Eleni's numbers, and does not appear in the results.

Accuracy is computed on all the items. Macro averages (macro F1) are computed on the classes with support in the gold, stating which classes are excluded and why; a prediction of a class without support remains an error.

P3. **Breakdowns by scheme and by corpus.** Every main result is reported by scheme and by corpus, in the same label space, with the number of items and of classes beside it: an accuracy on a corpus with few classes is not compared with one on a corpus with many classes without saying so. Corpora with fewer than 20 items have no breakdown of their own: they enter only the total, and the report states it with the name of the corpus and the number of items. Today, fine space with the collapsed one in parentheses: LOGIC 192 items and 18 classes (13), NLAS 197 and 16 (12), EthiX 144 and 10 (10), Reddit 67 and 4 (4); ElecDebate, with 1 item, is only in the total. By scheme: ad hominem 94, analogy 83, cause to effect 45, correlation to cause 11, example 86, expert opinion 78, popular opinion 109, slippery slope 95. The 20-item rule applies only to the corpora: every scheme is always reported, correlation to cause too with 11 items, with the number of items beside it, and the report flags every scheme with fewer than 30 items (today correlation to cause).

P4. **Ablation without NLAS.** Every main result is repeated without the NLAS items, a synthetic corpus generated by LLMs: 404 items.

P5. **Uncertainty and comparisons.** Bootstrap confidence intervals on accuracy and macro F1. The comparisons between aggregators are paired on the same items, with the paired bootstrap of P12 and the exact McNemar test as a check.

P6. **Second evaluation on the workbook.** The items of `items.csv` outside the test set that have a terminal verdict of Enrico in `annotations.csv` are a second evaluation set, with labels produced within the taxonomy of the diagrams: today 113 items, none with two different terminal verdicts. The `idk` verdicts and the out-of-scope ones do not enter. The 9 items with a starting label (`starting_label`) equal to `false_cause` are excluded too: those rows have no scheme, so the set of terminals on which to compute the partial credit of spec 01 does not exist. Reconstructible count: 170 items outside the test set with a verdict of Enrico, minus the `idk` ones leaves 122, minus the 9 `false_cause` ones leaves 113. Partial credit remains in use for `any_fallacy`.

P7. **Per CQ.** For every model: accuracy and calibration (Brier, ECE) against Dumitru's answers in `annotations.csv`, annotated according to the map; agreement between models with kappa, always read together with the distribution of the answers; share of "cannot be determined from the text".

P8. **Human agreement.** Krippendorff's alpha and kappa between Enrico, Eleni and Dumitru on the verdicts; Dumitru against Eleni per CQ on the sample annotated by Eleni; Dumitru's internal consistency on the ten per cent of the rows re-annotated blind. An empty cell is not a verdict (`docs/dati.md`).

P9. **Uncertainty from the justifications.** Estimate of the uncertainty from the justifications of the models (linguistic markers, similarity between models: Eleni's proposal) and its relation to human disagreement.

P10. **Success criterion.** It is fixed before writing the scorer and before looking at any result of the experiments, and it holds for the decision of 26 October (thesis plan, decision point).
* Baseline: A0, the fixed diagram with the hard answers of a single model, which reproduces the setting of the diagnosis.
* Deciding metric: accuracy in the fine space (P2) in the gold scheme condition (P1). The accuracy in the predicted scheme condition is computed with the same comparison and serves as confirmation.
* Comparison: paired on the same items of the test set of the experiments, between the aggregator and A0, with a confidence interval on the difference in accuracy (P5).
* An improvement is claimed only if the interval of the paired comparison excludes zero. An interval that contains zero is reported as the absence of a measurable difference, not as a small improvement.
* Reference model: the reference A0 is that of the model with the highest accuracy on the test set of the experiments, measured as A0 with the deciding metric. The model is chosen before computing the results of the aggregators, and the choice is written in the manifest of the evaluation; with equal accuracy, the first model name in alphabetical order wins.
* Diagnostic comparison: an aggregator that uses a single model is also compared with the A0 of the same model, with the same paired comparison, and the result is reported next to the first one. It does not decide success: it separates two causes. If the aggregator does not beat the A0 of its own model, the negative result comes from the aggregation; if it beats it but stays below the A0 of the best model, it comes from the choice of the model.

P11. **Abstention curves.**
* Confidence: for an aggregator that returns a distribution over the terminals (A2, A3, A4, and A5 when it returns a probability), the confidence of an item is the probability of the highest verdict. With a threshold, the aggregator gives a verdict on the items with confidence at least equal to the threshold and abstains on the others; coverage is the share of items on which it gives a verdict. A0 and A1 are a single point, at full coverage, except when the diagram stops on `idk` with the `stop` policy, which counts as an abstention. The margin between the two most probable verdicts is computed as a check.
* Axes: coverage on the x axis, from 0 to 1; on the y axis the accuracy on the items on which the aggregator gives a verdict (selective accuracy), in the fine space and in the gold scheme condition, as in P10. The curve sorts the items by decreasing confidence and takes the first k, for every k from 1 to N; with equal confidence the order is by `item_id`, so the curve is deterministic.
* Summary number: area under the error-versus-coverage curve (AURC), lower is better, with a confidence interval (P12).
* Comparison: two aggregators are compared at equal coverage, never at equal threshold, because their confidences do not have the same scale. Levels fixed before looking at the results: 100, 95, 90, 80 and 70 per cent; we stop at 70 because a system that gives up on half of the cases is not useful. At each level c every aggregator gives a verdict on its first round(c·N) items by confidence. The intervals of the difference in selective accuracy and of the difference in AURC come from the paired bootstrap of P12, which recomputes both curves on the same resamples; a difference is declared only if the interval excludes zero.
* Next to every curve, the composition of the abstained items: share of Good Argumentation, scheme, corpus.

P12. **Bootstrap.**
* 10000 resamples, 95 per cent percentile interval.
* A single seed, fixed in the configuration of the evaluation and written in the manifest. The indices of the resamples are generated once and hold for all the aggregators and all the models: this is what makes the comparisons paired.
* The unit is the item, without strata: the items of the test set of the experiments are resampled with replacement, and all the answers and verdicts of an item move together. The breakdowns too, by scheme and by corpus, are computed on the same resamples of the whole test set, so the number of items of a breakdown varies from one resample to another; a resample with no items of a breakdown does not contribute to the interval of that breakdown. Reason: stratifying would keep the number of items per scheme fixed, and the interval would not include the uncertainty on the sample size, which with the 11 items of correlation to cause is exactly what must be shown.
* The exact McNemar test remains as a check on the paired comparison of two accuracies at full coverage.
