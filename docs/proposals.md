* For the full parser (spec 05): at stage one the three probabilities of `summary.csv` have no meaning. `summarise` orients `p_logprob`, `p_verbal` and `p_sample` on the first admitted answer, which at stage two is `yes` (`positive` for ad hominem CQ1) and at stage one is the first scheme id, `ad_hominem`: at stage one they are the probability of that scheme, whatever the answer. On the pilot, 43 of the 50 stage-one rows of every entry have a `p_sample` other than the frequency of the hard answer over the five samples (section 9 of the pilot report); the other 7 are the items answered `ad_hominem`. `p_logprob` is empty on every stage-one row, and so are the `p_logprob_*` columns of `answers.csv`: `logprob_masses` computes the masses over the scheme ids, but `answers.csv` has columns only for the answers of stage two, so the masses are lost, while `logprob_partial` is still written (true on 464 of the 1000 stage-one rows). At stage one there is no yes to orient on: the full parser should write the masses over the schemes and give, per row, the probabilities of the hard answer, or a distribution over the schemes. Nothing reads the three columns at stage one today; the pilot report computes stage one from the answers. Not decided yet.
* For the full parser (spec 05): 5 of the 298 hard answers of `qwen3_8_27b` at sample 0, at temperature 0, are not the answer with the highest `p_logprob`, and none on the other three entries of the pilot (section 9 of the pilot report). In all five the answer given and the highest mass are less than 0.001 apart (for example `yes` 0.3534 and `no` 0.3533, answer `no`). To be checked on `raw.jsonl`, at the answer position of those five calls. Two causes are possible. In `logprob_masses` a token that is a prefix of two admitted answers is credited only to the first one it matches, in the order of the options: `n` goes to `no` and never to `na` (to `negative` for ad hominem CQ1). And a mass sums every token that begins an answer, while the choice at temperature 0 takes the single most probable token, so near a tie the two can differ with no error in the parser. If the token chosen has the highest logprob of its position, it is the second; if the difference comes from a shared prefix, the rule of the first match has to change (split the token between the answers it begins, or leave it out and mark the row `logprob_partial`). Not decided yet.
* Reduce further what is saved of the logprobs, if the stored form of spec 04, section 2.5, is not enough. The pilot left 13 GB of `raw.jsonl` and 15 GB of `cache.sqlite` for its 6960 calls, and the full run of phase 2 covers the 773 items of `items.csv`, not 50. The copy of the logprobs inside `raw` and the `bytes` fields are no longer saved, about 70 per cent less per line by the measure on the pilot. What is left is the block itself: every generated token carries its 20 alternatives (`top_logprobs` is 20 for every call), the reasoning tokens included, up to 8192 per call, while the parser reads only the `token` of every entry, to find the last `"answer": "` (or `"scheme": "`), and the alternatives at that one position. The cache also writes its JSON with a space after every separator. The options left, from the one that loses nothing to the one that loses most: (c) compress, gzip for `raw.jsonl` and zlib for the `response` column of the cache: nothing is lost, but the files can no longer be read as text, and the resume, which today skips a line cut off by a crash, has to tolerate a gzip member cut off in the same way; (d) keep the alternatives only at the answer token: the client would have to find the final object, that is, parse, against the separation between inference and parsing, and a later parser could not read another position, the `confidence` for example; (e) lower `top_logprobs`: it is a generation parameter, so it changes the cache key, and the fewer the alternatives, the more rows are `logprob_partial` (stage one has nine admitted answers). Of the three, only (c) loses nothing. Whatever is chosen applies to new runs: the files written before stay as they are and must stay readable, and section 2.5 of spec 04 changes with it. Not decided yet.
