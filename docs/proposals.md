* Reduce further what is saved of the logprobs, if the stored form of spec 04, section 2.5, is not enough. The pilot left 13 GB of `raw.jsonl` and 15 GB of `cache.sqlite` for its 6960 calls, and the full run of phase 2 covers the 773 items of `items.csv`, not 50. The copy of the logprobs inside `raw` and the `bytes` fields are no longer saved, about 70 per cent less per line by the measure on the pilot. What is left is the block itself: every generated token carries its 20 alternatives (`top_logprobs` is 20 for every call), the reasoning tokens included, up to 8192 per call, while the parser reads only the `token` of every entry, to find the last `"answer": "` (or `"scheme": "`), and the alternatives at that one position. The cache also writes its JSON with a space after every separator. The options left, from the one that loses nothing to the one that loses most: (c) compress, gzip for `raw.jsonl` and zlib for the `response` column of the cache: nothing is lost, but the files can no longer be read as text, and the resume, which today skips a line cut off by a crash, has to tolerate a gzip member cut off in the same way; (d) keep the alternatives only at the answer token: the client would have to find the final object, that is, parse, against the separation between inference and parsing, and a later parser could not read another position, the `confidence` for example; (e) lower `top_logprobs`: it is a generation parameter, so it changes the cache key, and the fewer the alternatives, the more rows are `logprob_partial` (stage one has nine admitted answers). Of the three, only (c) loses nothing. Whatever is chosen applies to new runs: the files written before stay as they are and must stay readable, and section 2.5 of spec 04 changes with it. Not decided yet.
